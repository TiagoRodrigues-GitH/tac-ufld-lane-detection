TAC-UFLD v0.4.0 · elas_pilot_v2

TAC-UFLD: temporal lane detection pilots

Can a lightweight lane detector that looks at the previous frames beat the single-frame UFLD baseline? UFLD (ResNet-18) and a small CNN, each with temporal variants, trained on the ELAS ego-lane dataset and tested on three road scenes never used for training or model selection.

What the results show

  • Geometric augmentation removes the UFLD overfitting. In the augmentation ablation (UFLD baseline, 3 seeds, same split and budget), small random shifts, zooms, rotations and perspective changes applied to the whole clip lift the best validation lane F1 from 0.161 to 0.772 and the held-out lane F1 from 0.068 to 0.858. The best epoch moves from 1 to 4, the last one. Photometric changes alone reach 0.150 held-out; flips add nothing (0.769). The recipe of the second pilot was chosen on validation: geometric augmentation plus current-frame degradation, for every model.
  • Dropout + label smoothing + weight decay together make UFLD worse (held-out 0.002). Lanes are still predicted in every frame but misplaced. The likely cause is label smoothing, which flattens the distribution over the 100 grid cells and pulls the soft-argmax towards the image centre; the arm changes three things at once, so this is a hypothesis.
  • With the new recipe the UFLD baseline reaches 0.881 held-out lane F1 (0.849 on seen scenes), against 0.057 in the first pilot. The second pilot trained 11 models × 2 seeds in 4 h 47 min on the RTX 3050.
  • The UFLD temporal models match the baseline but do not clearly beat it. v0.2, v0.3, v0.4 and v0.6 land between 0.872 and 0.883, the equal-training control at 0.883. UFLD v0.7 (ConvGRU) is highest at 0.895 (+0.014 over the baseline, +0.012 over the control), but replacing its history by the current frame at test time costs only 0.005, so that gain is not clearly temporal.
  • The lite models are under-trained after 4 epochs of the new recipe (they train from scratch): lite baseline 0.218, +CT 0.312. Lite v0.5 reaches 0.700 and lite v0.6 0.541, far above both, but replacing their history by the current frame costs only 0.020 and 0.017. Most of their lead therefore comes from the extra fusion layers, not from time. A longer lite run with a capacity control (lite v0.5 fed the current frame in every position) tests this.
  • The Kalman tracker gives the smoothness for free. On every model it lowers jitter by about 20 % (UFLD baseline 1.51 to 1.19 px) with unchanged lane F1. The learned temporal UFLD models are about as steady as their baseline (1.45 to 1.75 px against 1.51 px), so the tracker, not the fusion, is what makes the output smoother.
  • Carrying the recurrent state over a whole scene does not work yet. Trained on 3-frame clips, the ConvGRU drifts when its state is never reset: UFLD v0.7 loses 0.028 and lite v0.6 0.483 lane F1 in carry mode. One-state deployment needs training on long sequences (truncated back-propagation through time) first.
  • Occlusion is where time might help. In the one held-out scene tagged "occlusion" (VIX_S05), UFLD v0.7 scores 0.792 against 0.741 for the baseline and 0.772 for the equal-training control; in the other two scenes the baseline is best (0.948). One scene is anecdotal.
  • Cost. With cached history features each temporal UFLD model costs its baseline (7.1 GMACs per frame) plus the fusion; per 3-frame clip v0.7 needs 21.5 GMACs and 22.1 M parameters (+0.3 M), v0.6 21.8 GMACs and 23.5 M (+1.7 M). The lite models need 2.0 GMACs per frame and 2.5 to 2.8 M parameters.

The models

Two families, each a single-frame baseline and temporal variants built on it. All of them read the current frame and predict the two ego lanes of that frame; the temporal ones also read earlier frames.

  • Every temporal model keeps its baseline's backbone and head and only adds a fusion step between them. It is compared with its own baseline on the same seeds.
  • Three questions separate the temporal models: do they align the frames before fusing (v0.5, v0.6), how do they remember (a fixed 3-frame window for v0.2 to v0.6, a recurrent state for v0.7 and lite v0.6), and what do they cost (UFLD about 7 GMACs per frame, lite about 2).
  • With feature caching each frame is encoded once, so a temporal model costs its baseline plus the fusion step, not three backbones.
  • The controls are not candidates. +CT separates a temporal gain from extra training, lite v0.5 static separates it from the extra fusion layers, and the Kalman tracker shows what simple output filtering already gives.

UFLD family

UFLD baseline

baselineOfficial UFLD (Qin et al., ECCV 2020), supervisor's notebook21.78 M params · 7.1 GMACs per clip

What it doesUltra-Fast Lane Detection with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18. Lane detection is row-wise classification: for each of 18 image rows and each lane slot the head picks one of 100 horizontal cells, or "no lane". One frame in, no memory.

DifferenceThe reference of the UFLD family. Every UFLD temporal model starts from its trained weights and is compared with it on the same seeds.

UFLD baseline +CT

controlv0.4 control21.78 M params · 7.1 GMACs per clip

What it doesThe same network trained a second time from its own best checkpoint, with exactly the schedule the temporal variants get after their warm start.

DifferenceA control, not a candidate. A temporal model that beats the baseline but not this control gained from extra training, not from time.

UFLD v0.2 weighted

temporalSupervisor's notebook21.78 M params · 21.3 GMACs per clip, 3 frames per clip

What it doesEncodes the three frames with the shared ResNet and averages the feature maps with three learned weights (starting at 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.7) before the UFLD head. Trained with an extra existence loss and a temporal-consistency loss.

DifferenceThe simplest fusion: one weight per frame, the same at every pixel. Lane markings that moved between frames are averaged at different positions.

UFLD v0.3 gated

temporalSupervisor's notebook21.91 M params · 21.4 GMACs per clip, 3 frames per clip

What it doesLike v0.2, but a small convolutional network predicts a softmax gate per frame and per feature cell, biased towards the current frame.

DifferenceCan use history only where the current frame is weak, such as an occluded cell. Like v0.2 it fuses feature maps that are not aligned.

UFLD v0.4 coord

temporalSupervisor's notebook21.78 M params · 21.3 GMACs per clip, 3 frames per clip

What it doesv0.2's network with a different loss: a soft-argmax coordinate term penalises the lane position error in pixels, and the existence loss is re-weighted.

DifferenceSame fusion as v0.2. It tests whether a position-aware loss helps; the network itself is unchanged.

UFLD v0.6 aligned

temporalNew in v0.4 (port of lite v0.5)23.50 M params · 21.8 GMACs per clip, 3 frames per clip

What it doesA learned flow field (up to 4 cells of the 12x16 feature grid, about 128 px) warps each history map onto the current frame. The aligned history is compressed and blended in through a per-cell gate that starts almost closed.

DifferenceThe only UFLD model that aligns frames before fusing, so moving markings are not blurred. It adds about 1.7 M parameters to the baseline.

UFLD v0.7 ConvGRU

temporalNew in v0.422.07 M params · 21.5 GMACs per clip, 3 frames per clip

What it doesA convolutional GRU (64 channels) runs over the reduced feature maps from the oldest frame to the current one. Its final state is projected back and added to the current features. The projection starts at zero, so the untrained model is exactly the baseline.

DifferenceRemembers through a gated recurrence instead of a weighted sum. A deployment can carry one hidden state per camera instead of storing past feature maps.

Lite family

Lite baseline

baselineELAS development script2.49 M params · 2.0 GMACs per clip

What it doesA small CNN: four stride-2 convolution blocks (32 to 128 channels) and a head that pools the features to the 18x100 row-anchor grid and classifies every row with a shared MLP. Trained from scratch, without ImageNet.

DifferenceThe reference of the lite family: about 8 times fewer parameters and 3 to 4 times less compute than UFLD.

Lite baseline +CT

controlv0.4 control2.49 M params · 2.0 GMACs per clip

What it doesThe lite baseline trained a second time from its own best checkpoint with the temporal variants' schedule.

DifferenceThe equal-training control for lite v0.5 and lite v0.6.

Lite v0.5 warped

temporalELAS development script (refactored)2.84 M params · 6.0 GMACs per clip, 3 frames per clip

What it doesThe lite baseline plus warped residual fusion: a learned flow (up to 8 cells at stride 16, about 128 px) aligns each history feature map to the current one, and a per-pixel gate that starts closed mixes the aligned history in.

DifferenceSame fusion idea as UFLD v0.6 on a network that is 8 times smaller.

Lite v0.6 ConvGRU

temporalNew in v0.42.73 M params · 6.1 GMACs per clip, 3 frames per clip

What it doesThe lite baseline plus the ConvGRU fusion of UFLD v0.7 (64 hidden channels, read-out starting at zero).

DifferenceRecurrent memory on the smallest network: the candidate for an embedded system that keeps one state tensor per camera. Unlike v0.5 it does not align frames explicitly.

Reference, not a model: Any model + Kalman tracker: a causal constant-velocity Kalman filter on every lane point, with existence smoothing, tuned on validation. It costs microseconds per frame and is the bar a learned temporal model has to clear.

Lane F1 on unseen roads

CULane-style lane F1: each lane drawn 12 px wide (30 px at 1640 px, scaled to 640 px), matched one-to-one, correct when IoU ≥ 0.5. Post-processing tuned on validation only. On ELAS every model predicts both ego lanes in every frame, so precision = recall = F1: the errors are misplaced lanes, not missed ones.

Roadmap

First make a lightweight temporal model beat the UFLD baseline under the full protocol; only then optimise for an in-car embedded system.

Phase 1: protocol and first pilot (v0.3)

  • doneLeakage-safe ELAS protocol: three held-out road scenes, 60-frame blocks with a purge gap, six seeds, HPO with the same budget for every model, validation-only selection.
  • doneGPU pilot (2 seeds, up to 4 epochs): lite v0.5 is best on unseen roads; every UFLD run peaks at epoch 1 and then overfits; history adds little to any model.
  • doneDeployment tooling: ONNX, TensorRT FP32 / FP16 / INT8 on the desktop GPU, streaming with cached history features.

Phase 2: make a lightweight temporal model beat the UFLD baseline (v0.4)

  • doneFix the UFLD overfitting: geometric augmentation lifts the UFLD baseline from 0.07 to 0.86 held-out lane F1 (configs/ablations/augmentation.yaml, chosen on validation); extended photometric augmentation, flips and dropout + label smoothing do not help.
  • in progressThe other overfitting levers as single changes to the original recipe: a 10x lower backbone learning rate, frozen early layers, a smaller UFLD head, current-frame degradation and 8 more ELAS training scenes (remaining arms of the same ablation). Cross-dataset initialisation (model.init_checkpoint) is implemented for when CULane / TuSimple weights are available.
  • in progressLonger history: grid of frame steps 1 to 15 and 2 to 5 frames with one shared leakage-safe split (configs/ablations/history.yaml).
  • doneCurrent-frame degradation: only the current frame is occluded, blurred, darkened or made noisy while the history stays clean, so a temporal model must use its history (data.augmentation.current_frame_prob).
  • doneAlign before fusing: UFLD v0.6 (lite v0.5's learned warp and gate on UFLD features).
  • doneRecurrent state: UFLD v0.7 and lite v0.6 (ConvGRU); streaming can carry one state per camera (--mode carry).
  • doneEqual-training controls: the +CT baselines get the same extra training as the temporal models.
  • doneEvaluate where time should help: lane F1 per scene condition, jitter, and a validation-tuned Kalman tracker as the cheap temporal reference.
  • doneSecond pilot with the v0.4 models and recipe (configs/elas_pilot_v2.yaml): UFLD baseline 0.881, best temporal UFLD (v0.7) 0.895; the lite models need more epochs.
  • doneCapacity control lite_v05_static: lite v0.5 fed the current frame in every position, to separate a temporal gain from the extra fusion layers.
  • in progressLite family with a longer budget (up to 16 epochs) and the capacity control (configs/elas_lite_long.yaml).
  • nextTrain the recurrent models on long sequences so that one carried state per camera works (carry mode drifts after 3-frame training).

Phase 3: full protocol (supervisor)

  • nextFull run: 6 seeds, HPO, up to 50 epochs, with the recipe and models chosen from the pilots (configs/elas.yaml).
  • laterPretraining on CULane / TuSimple, then fine-tuning on ELAS (needs the datasets or the official UFLD weights).

Phase 4: embedded automotive deployment (last step)

  • laterMeasure on the real device: build the TensorRT engines on a Jetson and run the measurement script; today's Jetson figures are simulations with assumed slowdowns.
  • laterTake preprocessing off the CPU: the CPU resize is about 9 ms of a 14 to 19 ms frame. Resize on the camera's image processor or the GPU with zero-copy buffers, and retrain with that same resize.
  • laterCrop to the road: the row anchors cover only the lower 41 % of the image, so cropping cuts compute by about 2.4 times; a lower input resolution is the second option. Both need retraining.
  • laterTensorRT FP16 by default (lossless in the pilot); INT8 only if FP16 is too slow, calibrated on the car's camera or with quantization-aware training for the lite models.
  • laterPrefer the lite family: about 8 times fewer parameters and 3.6 times less compute than UFLD.
  • laterRobustness to dropped frames: train with frame drops and space the history by timestamps, since the car's camera may run at a different frame rate than ELAS.
  • laterProduction runtime: C++ TensorRT or DeepStream instead of Python, capture / inference / post-processing in parallel, always process the latest frame, locked clocks, thermal tests inside the enclosure.
