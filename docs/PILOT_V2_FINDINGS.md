# v0.4 findings: augmentation ablation and second pilot (2026-09-30)

Indicative only: 2 to 3 seeds, at most 4 epochs, no hyper-parameter search, so every paired test is
`underpowered`. The list items below are shown on the results page
(`python -m tac_ufld site --runs results/elas_pilot_v2 --notes docs/PILOT_V2_FINDINGS.md`).

- **Geometric augmentation removes the UFLD overfitting.** In the augmentation ablation (UFLD baseline, 3 seeds, same split and budget), small random shifts, zooms, rotations and perspective changes applied to the whole clip lift the best validation lane F1 from 0.161 to 0.772 and the held-out lane F1 from 0.068 to 0.858. The best epoch moves from 1 to 4, the last one. Photometric changes alone reach 0.150 held-out; flips add nothing (0.769). The recipe of the second pilot was chosen on validation: geometric augmentation plus current-frame degradation, for every model.
- **Dropout + label smoothing + weight decay together make UFLD worse** (held-out 0.002). Lanes are still predicted in every frame but misplaced. The likely cause is label smoothing, which flattens the distribution over the 100 grid cells and pulls the soft-argmax towards the image centre; the arm changes three things at once, so this is a hypothesis.
- **With the new recipe the UFLD baseline reaches 0.881 held-out lane F1** (0.849 on seen scenes), against 0.057 in the first pilot. The second pilot trained 11 models × 2 seeds in 4 h 47 min on the RTX 3050.
- **The UFLD temporal models match the baseline but do not clearly beat it.** v0.2, v0.3, v0.4 and v0.6 land between 0.872 and 0.883, the equal-training control at 0.883. UFLD v0.7 (ConvGRU) is highest at 0.895 (+0.014 over the baseline, +0.012 over the control), but replacing its history by the current frame at test time costs only 0.005, so that gain is not clearly temporal.
- **The lite models are under-trained after 4 epochs of the new recipe** (they train from scratch): lite baseline 0.218, +CT 0.312. Lite v0.5 reaches 0.700 and lite v0.6 0.541, far above both, but replacing their history by the current frame costs only 0.020 and 0.017. Most of their lead therefore comes from the extra fusion layers, not from time.
- **With 16 epochs the lite family comes close to UFLD, with 8× fewer parameters.** The longer lite run (`elas_lite_long`, 2 seeds, early stopping) puts lite v0.5 at 0.850 held-out lane F1 (0.844 / 0.856) with 2.8 M parameters, against 0.881 for the UFLD baseline with 21.8 M. The lite baseline stays at 0.322 and more training does not help it (+CT 0.322). The capacity control (lite v0.5 with the current frame in every position, the same layers) is unstable: 0.877 in seed 1 and 0.627 in seed 2. So the share of lite v0.5's gain that comes from the earlier frames is still open (+0.098 on average, −0.033 and +0.229 per seed). Lite v0.6 (ConvGRU) reaches 0.561.
- **The Kalman tracker gives the smoothness for free.** On every model it lowers jitter by about 20 % (UFLD baseline 1.51 to 1.19 px) with unchanged lane F1. The learned temporal UFLD models are about as steady as their baseline (1.45 to 1.75 px against 1.51 px), so the tracker, not the fusion, is what makes the output smoother.
- **Carrying the recurrent state over a whole scene does not work yet.** Trained on 3-frame clips, the ConvGRU drifts when its state is never reset: UFLD v0.7 loses 0.028 and lite v0.6 0.483 lane F1 in carry mode. One-state deployment needs training on long sequences (truncated back-propagation through time) first.
- **Occlusion is where time might help.** In the one held-out scene tagged "occlusion" (VIX_S05), UFLD v0.7 scores 0.792 against 0.741 for the baseline and 0.772 for the equal-training control; in the other two scenes the baseline is best (0.948). One scene is anecdotal.
- **When the current frame is occluded, the temporal models keep the lanes.** The robustness evaluation (`python -m tac_ufld robustness`) re-ran every checkpoint on the 2,610 held-out frames. In each frame the current image was occluded by boxes, blurred, darkened or made noisy, while the history frames stayed clean; every model saw the same corruptions. Under occlusion (673 frames), UFLD v0.3 keeps 0.875 lane F1 (seeds 0.865 / 0.886) and v0.6 0.864 (0.857 / 0.871), against 0.761 for the baseline (0.753 / 0.768) and 0.742 for the equal-training control. That is +0.11 in both seeds, so more training alone does not close the gap. Over all degraded frames, v0.3 reaches 0.886 and v0.6 0.884 against 0.850 for the baseline. For v0.3 and v0.6 that is marginally above their clean-frame F1 (0.883 and 0.880). Blur, darkening and noise barely hurt any UFLD model (within ±0.03). v0.7 gains less under occlusion (0.802), and the lite models lose 0.02–0.07 like their baselines. The Kalman tracker adds nothing here. Caveats: 2 seeds, one fixed set of synthetic corruptions, and the models were trained with current-frame degradation, so this measures what that training teaches, not natural occlusions.
- **Looking further back does not help; looking at more frames hurts.** The history ablation (`ablation_history`, UFLD v0.4, the temporal model chosen on pilot-2 validation, 2 seeds) uses its own split with 180-frame blocks, where the baseline scores 0.831. It gains +0.029 over the baseline with 3 frames 2 apart, +0.028 with 5 apart and +0.027 with 10 apart, but only +0.004 with 5 frames 5 apart. The spacing of the history barely matters in this range; a longer window makes v0.4 worse.
- **Cost.** With cached history features each temporal UFLD model costs its baseline (7.1 GMACs per frame) plus the fusion; per 3-frame clip v0.7 needs 21.5 GMACs and 22.1 M parameters (+0.3 M), v0.6 21.8 GMACs and 23.5 M (+1.7 M). The lite models need 2.0 GMACs per frame and 2.5 to 2.8 M parameters.
