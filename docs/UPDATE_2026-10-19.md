# Update 2026-10-19: degradation sequences, every model, and the decision for the full run

Three parts: (1) is it scientifically worth corrupting whole sequences (blur, darkening, noise over several
frames) instead of one frame; (2) what each of the 19 models is, does and costs; (3) pilot results and the
decision for the full training. Numbers are from 1-seed, 4-epoch pilots: indicative, no significance.

## 1. Corrupting sequences, not one frame: is it relevant for a paper?

**Yes, and it is necessary, not optional.** Three reasons:

1. **Realism.** With `temporal_step: 2` the history covers t-2 and t-4, i.e. 133 ms at 29.97 FPS. Night, rain,
   a tunnel, sun glare, a truck ahead or a dirty lens last seconds, so every frame of the window is corrupted.
   Corrupting only the current frame models a momentary fault (one dropped, flashed or occluded frame), and it
   favours temporal models by construction: their history is clean. A reviewer from the robustness community
   will ask for the persistent case.
2. **It changes the conclusions.** In the TSM pilot (training corrupted only the current frame), lite_tsm was the
   best model when only the current frame was corrupted (0.931), and the **worst** under persistent corruption
   (0.775, below the single-frame baseline's 0.795). A paper that reported only the first protocol would claim a
   robustness gain that reverses in the realistic case.
3. **The literature treats video robustness as its own problem.** Yi et al. (NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and
   Benchmarks) built video corruption benchmarks (Mini Kinetics-C, Mini SSV2-C) with temporal corruptions, and
   found that robustness remedies from images (e.g. training with noise) do not necessarily carry over to
   spatio-temporal models. Hendrycks and Dietterich (ICLR 2019) measure the stability of predictions along
   *sequences* of perturbations (ImageNet-P, flip probability); their noise sequences have independent frames.
   In a quick search I did not find a temporal-corruption protocol for video lane detection. Check this before
   claiming it as a contribution. Neither paper is in `references.bib` yet; both were checked online
   (arXiv 2110.06513; arXiv 1903.12261).

### The scientific framing: four corruption regimes

The useful question is not "does the temporal model resist corruption" but "**in which regime can the history
help, and does the model know when it cannot?**"

| regime | robustness protocol | what the history holds | what a good temporal model should do |
|---|---|---|---|
| momentary fault (one bad frame) | `current` | clean evidence | use it: large gain |
| lasting condition, independent per frame (fresh sensor noise, moving occluders, fluctuating blur / light) | `independent` (new today) | complementary evidence | moderate gain (it can aggregate) |
| lasting condition, identical on every frame (the same occluder box, the same noise sample) | `persistent` | the same corrupted evidence | nothing to gain: **must not be worse than single-frame** |
| corruption never seen in training (cast shadow, glare) | `held_out` | clean evidence | generalise |

The claim a paper can defend then reads: *the temporal model gains in the momentary and independent regimes, and
does not lose in the identical-persistent regime.* The last part is a non-inferiority test against the
single-frame baseline (and against the capacity control).

Note on realism: sensor noise is independent from frame to frame, so the `persistent` noise case (one noise
pattern frozen on every frame) is a worst case rather than a realistic one; the realistic noise case is
`independent`. For darkening and blur, `persistent` is realistic (a tunnel, a dirty lens).

### What is implemented

* **Training:** `data.augmentation.current_frame_persistent_share` (pilot v3: 0.33). Of the 30 % degraded clips,
  one third are degraded on every frame with the same parameters, two thirds on the current frame only. The
  model then sees both clean and corrupted history, and cannot learn to trust the history blindly.
* **Evaluation** (`python -m tac_ufld robustness`): `current`, `persistent`, `held_out` and, added today,
  `independent` (`degrade_current_frame(..., scope="independent")`, test in `tests/test_lite_tsm_occ.py`). It is
  evaluation-only, so it needs no retraining and applies to every finished run.
* **Not implemented** (options, in order of value): an *onset* regime (a corruption that starts inside the window,
  e.g. entering a tunnel: the oldest frames are clean); training with independent sequences; a longer history
  (`temporal_step: 10` = 0.67 s; review R1), which is the only way for the history to outlast short occlusions.

### UFLD family (ResNet-18, ImageNet-pretrained)

* **ufld_baseline**: official Ultra-Fast Lane Detection (Qin et al., ECCV 2020): ResNet-18, a global
  fully connected head over the whole feature map, one frame. The reference that the article must beat.
* **ufld_baseline_ct**: the same network trained again from its own best checkpoint with the temporal models'
  schedule. A temporal model warm-starts from the baseline and then trains more epochs; if it beats the baseline
  but not +CT, the gain is extra training, not time.
* **ufld_v02** (supervisor's notebook): the three frames go through the shared backbone; their feature maps are
  averaged with three learned weights before the head. Simplest fusion: one weight per frame, the same at every
  pixel, no alignment, so markings that moved are blurred together.
* **ufld_v03** (supervisor's notebook): a small CNN predicts a softmax gate per frame and per feature cell, so the
  history can be used only where the current frame is weak. Still unaligned.
* **ufld_v04** (supervisor's notebook): v0.2's network with a soft-argmax coordinate loss and re-weighted existence
  loss: tests the loss, not the architecture.
* **ufld_v06**: a learned flow field warps each history map onto the current frame, then a per-cell gate that
  starts nearly closed mixes it in. The only UFLD model that aligns frames; it costs +1.7 M parameters.
* **ufld_v07**: a ConvGRU (64 channels) runs over the frames; its state is projected back and added to the current
  features, the projection starts at zero (the untrained model is the baseline). Can be deployed with one state
  per camera ("carry").
* **ufld_v08**: every current cell attends to the cells of the three frames (4 heads, keys and values pooled), the
  approach of TGC-Net / LaneTCA; zero-initialised read-out.

### Lite family (4-block CNN, trained from scratch; 8.7x fewer parameters, 3.6x fewer MACs than UFLD)

* **lite_baseline**: four stride-2 convolution blocks (32 to 128 channels) and a head that reads the features at
  each row anchor's own image row (`AnchorRowPool`, the 2026-10-18 fix) and classifies each row with a shared MLP.
* **lite_baseline_ct**: the equal-training control of the lite family (see ufld_baseline_ct). In pilot v3 it is
  essential: see section 3.
* **lite_v05**: lite baseline + warped residual fusion (the UFLD v0.6 idea): learned flow up to 8 cells
  (~128 px), per-pixel gate starting closed.
* **lite_v05_static**: lite v0.5 exactly, but every history frame is the current frame, in training and test. A
  capacity control: if v0.5 beats it, the gain comes from the past frames and not from the extra layers.
* **lite_v06**: lite baseline + the ConvGRU of UFLD v0.7. The embedded candidate with one small state per camera.
* **lite_v06_occ** (new, idea of OMR, ECCV 2024, and SIGMA-Lane): lite v0.6 plus one 3x3 convolution that
  estimates, per cell, how visible the incoming frame is; the GRU update gate is multiplied by it, so a cell that
  is occluded, blurred or dark keeps its memory instead of overwriting it. The visibility has no label; it is
  learned through the lane loss and the degraded training clips. +577 parameters.
* **lite_v07**: lite baseline + the cross-frame attention of UFLD v0.8 (keys and values pooled to 12x20 cells).
* **lite_tsm** (new; TSM, Lin et al., ICCV 2019, online edge version): the lite baseline's *exact* layers; at the
  input of blocks 2, 3 and 4, 1/8 of the channels are replaced by the same channels of the previous frame, so every
  block mixes time with its ordinary convolutions. **Zero extra parameters and, in carry mode, zero extra MACs**
  (one baseline pass per frame; state 1.27 MiB per stream chain). Its memory is finite: carry mode equals a
  4-frame window (one frame per shifted block plus the current one). Unlike the fusion models it does not start
  as its baseline after the warm start.
* **lite_tsm_static**: the capacity control of lite_tsm (history = current frame). The shift alone changes the
  network (channel mixing, zero padding), so this control is needed to attribute a gain to time.
* **lite_tsm_kd**: lite_tsm trained with an extra loss that matches its per-anchor distribution to the softened
  distribution of the trained UFLD v0.7 temporal teacher (Hinton et al. 2015). Reference: lite_tsm, so the pair
  isolates the distillation.
* **lite_baseline_kd**: the single-frame lite model taught by the same temporal teacher, which sees t-4, t-2, t
  while the student sees only t: can temporal knowledge be distilled into a model with no history at all?

### Controls and references, summarised

| control | answers | rule |
|---|---|---|
| `*_baseline_ct` | is the gain extra training? | a temporal model must beat +CT, not only the baseline |
| `*_static` | is the gain extra layers? | a temporal model must beat its static twin |
| Kalman tracker on any model | is a learned temporal model better than output filtering? | the learned model must beat baseline + Kalman |
| `static_history` input (evaluation) | does the trained temporal model use its history? | its F1 should drop when the history is replaced by the current frame |

What the clean frames say (1 seed; differences below about 0.01 are within seed noise):

1. **The equal-training control changes the lite story.** lite_baseline_ct (0.935) is as good as lite_v05 (0.934)
   and close to every lite temporal model. With 4 epochs the lite baseline is under-trained, so most of the lite
   "temporal gain" over lite_baseline (0.901) in this and the earlier pilots is extra training. Over +CT, the best
   models gain +0.008 (lite_tsm) and +0.006 (lite_v06): too small to claim on clean frames with one seed.
2. **Which models really use their history** (F1 drops when it is removed): lite_tsm (-0.028), lite_tsm_kd
   (-0.032), lite_v06 (-0.019), lite_v06_occ (-0.019), lite_v05 (-0.012), ufld_v06 (-0.022). lite_v07 and ufld_v08
   do not use it at all (no drop): the attention models' gain over their baseline is capacity or training, not time.
3. **Carry mode:** lite_tsm loses nothing (0.943, its memory is finite), the ConvGRUs lose 0.015 (lite) to 0.033
   (UFLD) when their state is carried beyond the 3-frame training window (review R2).
4. **Light vs UFLD:** every lite model beats every UFLD model on the held-out scenes (0.90-0.94 vs 0.85-0.89) with
   8.7x fewer parameters; on the seen-test scenes (roads seen in training) UFLD is slightly ahead (0.84-0.86 vs
   0.82-0.85). UFLD generalises worse to new roads.
5. **Distillation** from UFLD v0.7 does not help here: lite_tsm_kd 0.936 < lite_tsm 0.943, and lite_baseline_kd
   0.925 < lite_baseline_ct 0.935. The teacher (0.878) is weaker than the students.
6. **Lane-change false alarms** (few events, indicative): lite_tsm 2.1 / min, lite_v06_occ 1.4 / min, most lite
   models 0.7 / min, UFLD 0. This is open problem 2 of `docs/LITERATURE_2026-10-19.md` (detector thresholds).

What the robustness says (1 seed):

1. **The persistent-share recipe fixed the TSM collapse.** lite_tsm under persistent corruption: 0.775 with the
   old recipe -> 0.855 now (lite_baseline_ct 0.859: non-inferior within noise), with no clean-frame cost (0.938 ->
   0.943). The regime framing of section 1 is confirmed: momentary faults, gain +0.073 over +CT; independent
   lasting conditions, +0.027; identical persistent, -0.004 (no gain, no loss); unseen corruptions, +0.123.
2. **lite_v06 (ConvGRU) is the most robust model of the study**: best under independent (0.919, +0.060 over +CT;
   with moving occluders 0.892 vs 0.714) and persistent corruption (0.870, +0.011), third on held-out (0.875, after
   the two TSM models). It aggregates evidence over frames; TSM only shifts a slice of the previous frame.
3. **lite_v06_occ = lite_v06** within noise on every protocol: the unsupervised visibility gate brings nothing
   measurable. It would need a supervised visibility target (e.g. the occlusion boxes of the degradation) to be a
   contribution.
4. **The best light model beats the best UFLD model on every protocol**: lite_v06 vs the best UFLD value of each
   column, clean 0.941 vs 0.891, current 0.926 vs 0.866, independent 0.919 vs 0.866, persistent 0.870 vs 0.856,
   held-out 0.875 vs 0.799. lite_tsm ties the best UFLD under persistent corruption (0.855 vs 0.856). Among UFLD
   models ufld_v07 is the most robust; **ufld_v03, the declared primary of `elas_full22`, is below its baseline on
   clean, persistent and held-out frames.**
5. **The models that do not use their history (lite_v07, ufld_v08) gain nothing under corruption** either
   (lite_v07 = lite_baseline_ct on every protocol).
6. **Distillation**: lite_tsm_kd matches lite_tsm within noise; lite_baseline_kd is below lite_baseline_ct on
   clean and momentary corruption. Not worth the full-run budget.

**Caveat on trackers and carry mode (protocol limitation):** every test frame draws its own corruption (op and
parameters seeded by the frame index), so the corruption is persistent inside a model's 3-frame window but
independent from one test frame to the next. Anything with memory longer than the window (the Kalman tracker,
carry mode) therefore sees a "momentary" regime and looks too good: with the validation-tuned adaptive Kalman,
lite_baseline_ct rises from 0.859 to 0.927 under "persistent" corruption, almost the level of the best temporal
models. A fair test needs **corruption episodes**: the same condition over many consecutive test frames (e.g.
1-3 s), which is what "a sequence of blur, darkening and noise" means at the level of the video. It is
evaluation-only (no retraining) and should be added before the final evaluation of the full run.

## 4. Decision for the full run

Current `configs/elas_full22.yaml` (decided 2026-10-05): variants `ufld_baseline, ufld_v03, lite_baseline,
lite_v05, lite_v05_static`; primary pairs `[ufld_v03, ufld_baseline]`, `[lite_v05, lite_v05_static]`; 7 seeds,
50 epochs, HPO; current-frame degradation only. The pilot argues for four changes:

1. **Recipe:** add `data.augmentation.current_frame_persistent_share: 0.33` (pilot v3). Without it the best light
   model is worse than its baseline in the realistic regime.
2. **Variants:** `ufld_baseline, ufld_v07, lite_baseline, lite_baseline_ct, lite_tsm, lite_tsm_static, lite_v06`
   (7 instead of 5). ufld_v07 replaces ufld_v03 (best UFLD temporal model; v03 is below its baseline). lite_tsm
   (zero-cost, best clean / momentary / held-out) and lite_v06 (best under lasting corruption) replace lite_v05,
   which both dominate on every protocol. lite_baseline_ct is mandatory: without it the lite temporal gains are
   overstated by about 0.03. Dropped: v0.2 / v0.4 / v0.6 / v0.8, lite_v05(_static), lite_v06_occ, lite_v07 and
   the KD students (no gain over a cheaper model, or no use of the history). Optional if the budget allows:
   ufld_baseline_ct (the pilot shows no CT effect for UFLD, 0.858 vs 0.864).
3. **Primary pairs** (2, so that 7 seeds can pass Holm): `[lite_tsm, lite_baseline_ct]` (the article's claim:
   a zero-cost temporal model beats the equal-training single-frame model) and `[ufld_v07, ufld_baseline]`.
   Everything else is reported as exploratory, including `[lite_tsm, lite_tsm_static]` and lite_v06.
4. **Evaluation:** report all four protocols plus corruption episodes (to be added; evaluation-only), with the
   non-inferiority reading of the persistent protocol.

Cost: 7 variants instead of 5 is about +40 % GPU time over the original estimate (~6 GPU-days on the RTX 3050 for
5 variants, so roughly 8-9 GPU-days, HPO included), or the cluster copy `configs/cluster/elas_full22.yaml`
(batch 32, `ufld_aux: true`; do not mix its results with desktop runs). Two settings should be decided before
launching: where to run it, and whether to try `temporal_step: 10` first (review R1, a 1-seed pilot of about
2 h): the persistent results show the 133 ms history cannot outlast a lasting condition.
